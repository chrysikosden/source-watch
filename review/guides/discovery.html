Start in the right search space

Open Discovery, enter a Discovery query and choose a Search scope. The three controls answer different questions.

Choose how far to look
ControlSearchesUseful when…
Latest feedThe loaded recent feed from monitored channelsYou want a relevant recent upload among your sources.
Monitored historyVideos already indexed from monitored channelsYou want older material beyond the current feed.
Wider YouTubeYouTube search through its serviceYou want sources outside your monitored set.

Monitored history is only as broad as the metadata you have indexed. Use Channel Videos history controls when you deliberately want more older uploads. Wider YouTube uses service access and API quota; it is not an unlimited or complete index.

Make the next search purposeful

Search mode offers More evidence, Challenge this and Better source. These help aim your search toward supporting/related material, counterarguments or stronger source candidates. Build query from selection provides a starting point from selected material; review the query rather than treating it as the only possible framing.

Narrow by Category, Channel priority, Upload date (including an exact range), Length and whether Channels are all, new sources only or monitored only. Hide/include Shorts, then choose Cards or Grid for results.

A useful habit

First search the claim's own terms. Then try an alternative explanation, a conflicting definition or evidence that would change your mind. Save the promising sources to investigate—not just the ones that agree.

A score is a triage signal

Sort can use YouTube relevance, Research score, Newest or Views. The “Objective Discovery” heading is the application's label; it is not a certification of neutral, unbiased or verified truth.

Research scoring uses metadata heuristics such as query terms, title/description signals, recency and hype language. It can be wrong. Channel priority is your preference, not a credibility judgment. Popularity is not evidence that an argument is sound.

Trending asks what is popular

Open Trending, choose a Country and optionally a YouTube category, then select Refresh popular videos. Length and Shorts filters help narrow the result.

This view uses YouTube's current most-popular results for the selected country. It is not TubeUnpack's own trend prediction, a complete trend history or a ranking of trustworthy claims. Official YouTube categories are different from your local channel categories.

Country exploration asks a broader question

Explore by Country goes beyond the popular chart. Choose a country and Topic Paths such as economy, politics/geopolitics, technology/AI, energy or culture, then set Language, Upload date and Depth.

Explore country runs wider YouTube searches with the selected country in the query. Language can be Any language, English preferred or Local / mixed. Shallow, Medium and Deep change how far the search goes; deeper searches use more API quota, so this action is manual.

A country-related result is not proof that a creator lives there, that the video represents local opinion, or that it includes every relevant perspective. Language preference is a search aid—not guaranteed linguistic or geographic verification.

Diversity helps you ask better questions

Different origins, dates, methods and viewpoints can expose assumptions that one source leaves hidden. But several videos repeating the same underlying story are not independent corroboration.

Open promising sources, check their descriptions and available transcripts, and compare the evidence. When appropriate, gather them in a Research Project and prepare an attributed comparison package for your chosen assistant. Record what remains uncertain.

Source discovery is the beginning of verification work, not a replacement for it.
