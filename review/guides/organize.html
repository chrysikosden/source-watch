Organize / Build a focused feed

Your channels.
A clearer place to start.

Import the sources you follow, group them by topic and open the feed through that topic. Organizing locally does not rearrange your YouTube subscriptions.

Import Subscriptions → My Channels → Latest Long-form

Choose what enters your workspace

After connecting YouTube, open Channels → Import Subscriptions. Choose Load subscriptions if needed, then find and review the channels you want to monitor.

  1. Start with a small selection

    Use the channel checkboxes or Select visible. Check the selected count, then choose Import selected channels. Changing how many channels are shown is not the same as importing them.

  2. Review categories

    Category choices appear beside the channels. Suggested categories are a starting point; uncertain channels can be Uncategorized. Review them before or after importing.

  3. Confirm larger imports deliberately

    The all-subscription import asks you to confirm the full loaded count. Large lists take time; progress reports actual channel counts. A visible failure offers Retry.

Import adds channels to TubeUnpack's local monitored sources. It does not subscribe you to additional YouTube channels. A feed refresh does not change subscriptions or playlists.

Give channels a shared topic

Open Channels → My Channels. The category checkboxes for a channel let you assign or remove its memberships. Use Add a category when an existing group is not the right fit.

Categories is the place to create, rename and manage groups. A channel may belong to more than one category, so a source that covers technology and economics does not need to be forced into a single topic.

A simple first pass

Pick a handful of channels you already know. Give them one useful shared category, then open that category in your feed. Expand the system only when it helps you find material.

While browsing a channel in Channel Videos, the Categories control also opens its category editor. Names and avatars open a recent-upload preview.

Open the topic, not the whole pile

Choose Latest Long-form, then select your topic in Category. The feed also has Channel, Channel priority, Sort, Status and Search controls. Use Reset filters if a previous choice hides what you expected.

Scan or read

Switch between Cards and Compact list. Latest Long-form centers longer videos; Shorts has its own view.

Refresh intentionally

Refresh feeds fetches new uploads from monitored channels. This is not a full-history download and does not perform a YouTube write action.

Priority means “important to me”

Channel priority offers Not rated, Very low, Low, Medium, High and Essential. Use it to filter or sort channels and their videos according to your own attention.

This is your preference, not TubeUnpack's assessment of quality, accuracy or credibility. An Essential channel can still be wrong; a low-priority channel can still have useful evidence.

See what you already kept

Set the feed's Status → Saved to see saved items within the current feed and filters. For the full local saved library, open Saved & Folders.

Save to folders… keeps videos locally; saving without a folder places them in All saved. A video may belong to several folders. YouTube Playlists is a separate view of the connected account's playlists—not another name for local folders.

Older uploads are a separate job

Channels → Channel Videos browses recent uploads and already indexed history. Opening the view does not automatically scan the entire channel.

Choose Load older videos for another batch. Under History options, Refresh recent uploads refreshes recent metadata; Index full history… asks for confirmation before scanning toward the earliest upload.

Metadata, not video files

History indexing stores local video information for browsing and searching. It does not download playable video files. Large channels consume time and YouTube API quota; progress can resume, and very large histories may need more than one run.

Removing a monitored channel stops local monitoring; it does not unsubscribe on YouTube. Saved metadata, notes, transcripts and analyses remain available.
